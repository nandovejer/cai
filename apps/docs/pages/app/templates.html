Each template is markup to copy into your application, with your own text in place of the example. They use the classes of core and app only (and site's search, where one goes): a piece that would need CSS of its own is a pattern, with its page, and the last section says what LUZ does not have yet.

The view's <h1> and its main action are the first row of the dashboard, never in the bar. The bar holds what is the same in every view.

App page

The whole page of an application: the skip link, core's sidebar beside a main area that holds the header and the dashboard.

  • .o-shell puts the sidebar in a column and .o-main beside it. At 768 px and below the sidebar is a drawer that the menu button opens, with no script.
  • The skip link goes first and its target is the <main>, so a keyboard user passes the sidebar and the bar.
  • Leave out <luz-search> if you do not load site. Core's script, if you load it, marks the current link of the sidebar and closes the drawer when a link is followed.

App with no sidebar

A small application with a handful of views needs no sidebar. Use core's .o-page in place of the shell, and its navbar in the bar for the links.

  • The menu button opens the navbar, which collapses its links into a panel at 768 px and below; from 769 px the button is not shown and the links sit in the row.
  • The product's name is the first link of the navbar, to the home view: .luz-app-header__product is for the sidebar's drawer and is not used here.
  • The bar's .o-row holds the menu button, the navbar and the actions, in that order.
HTML 
<body class="o-page">
  <a class="luz-skip-link" href="#main-content">Skip to content</a>
  <header class="luz-app-header o-sticky">
    <div class="o-row">
      <button class="luz-nav-toggle" type="button" popovertarget="app-nav" aria-label="Menu">…</button>
      <nav class="luz-navbar" id="app-nav" aria-label="Main" popover>
        <ul class="luz-navbar__list">
          <li><a class="luz-navbar__link" href="/" aria-current="page">Bodega</a></li>
          <li><a class="luz-navbar__link" href="/reports/">Reports</a></li>
        </ul>
      </nav>
      <div class="o-row luz-app-header__actions">…</div>
    </div>
  </header>
  <main class="luz-app-dashboard" id="main-content" tabindex="-1">…</main>
</body>

Empty dashboard

A view with nothing in it yet: one wide panel with a sentence and the main action as a link. The title row keeps its place, so the view does not jump when the first panel arrives.

  • Say what is missing and what to do about it, in one sentence: "No reports yet. Create your first report."
  • The action is a link to where the report is made. It is the view's one primary action: leave it out of the title row while the panel holds it.
HTML 
<main class="luz-app-dashboard" id="main-content" tabindex="-1">
  <div class="luz-app-dashboard__head">
    <h1 class="luz-app-dashboard__title">Reports</h1>
  </div>
  <ul class="o-grid o-grid--auto">
    <li class="luz-card luz-app-dashboard__panel luz-app-dashboard__panel--wide">
      <h2 class="luz-card__title">No reports yet</h2>
      <p class="luz-card__body">Create your first report to see it here.</p>
      <a class="luz-btn luz-btn--primary" href="/reports/new/">New report</a>
    </li>
  </ul>
</main>

What is not here

An application often asks for things LUZ does not have yet. They are not faked in the templates above.

  • An icon rail or a collapsible sidebar. The sidebar is core's: a column from 769 px and a drawer below.
  • Charts, statistics and a table of data with filters. A panel holds your own content, a table or a progress bar.
  • Notifications, a workspace switcher and breadcrumbs in the bar. The bar holds the tools that are the same in every view.
