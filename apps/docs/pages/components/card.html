Known issues

In a linked card, the body text cannot be selected with a mouse: the title link covers the whole card, so a drag follows or moves the link. Find in page, keyboard selection and screen readers reach the text as usual.

Running
  • Linked card: the <a> goes inside the heading, round its text. The rest of the card stays text.
  • Avatar or icon: .o-media puts it beside .o-media__body, which holds the title and the body. Next to a name, hide the avatar with aria-hidden="true": it repeats the name.
  • Image on top: the image frame of @cai-ds/platform (.cai-platform-image), inside the padding, with .u-mb-sm under it. Its alt text says what the picture shows; if it only repeats the title, leave it empty (alt=""). With core alone, use an <img> and your own size rules.
  • Actions: .o-row with small buttons (.cai-btn--sm) or tags (.cai-tag), last in the card. Only in a card that is not linked.

How it works

A container styled by components/card.css. No JavaScript.

  • Use any element: <article> for a self-contained item, <div> for a group of facts, <li> inside a list of cards.
  • The title class goes on the heading level that fits the page outline.
  • The last title or body has no space under it: the padding is the card's last space.
  • A linked card: the link's ::after is stretched over the card (position: absolute; inset: 0), so a click anywhere in it follows the link. The card is position: relative to hold it. On hover the border turns --cai-input-border and a small shadow shows; on keyboard focus a ring goes round the card. The hover is found with :has(); a card without a link does not change on hover.
  • To lay cards out, use the grid object or the feature grid.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: a card, linked or not, is HTML and CSS.

Keyboard and ARIA

A card is not interactive and takes no focus.

  • Links and buttons inside it keep their own behaviour.
  • A linked card has one tab stop, its title link. Enter follows it. The focus ring shows round the link and round the whole card.
  • A screen reader names the link with the title, then reads the body as text. Add no role, tabindex or aria-label to the card.
  • Put the link on the title text, never round the card: a link round the card takes every word in it as its name.

Design

When to use

Use a card to group a small set of facts that read as one unit, often in a grid of similar items.

When not to use

Do not use a card to box content that is not a separate thing.

  • For rows of records with the same attributes, use a table.
  • For a message about the page, use an alert.
  • For a list of links, each with one line of description, use a plain list (<ul>): a card per link adds borders and nothing else.
  • Do not put a card inside a card.

Do and don't

Writing the content

A short noun as the title, then one or two lines.

  • No full stop on the title.
  • Keep cards in one group parallel: the same kind of title, the same length of body.
  • In a linked card, the title is the link text: it says where the link goes, so the card needs no “Read more”.

Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings, borders and icons.

  • Focus: a plain card takes none; the links and buttons inside keep their own ring. In a linked card the title link keeps its ring, and a second one, 2 px of --cai-focus-ring, goes round the card, 2 px outside its border. Both are outlines, so they stay in forced-colors mode.
  • Edges: the border (--cai-divider) is decorative and has no minimum: the content and the space around it group the card. On a linked card the hover border is --cai-input-border, 3:1 or more, with a shadow.
