Copy the markup

<figure class="cai-figure">
  <div class="cai-figure__media">
    <img src="requests.png" width="640" height="192" alt="Bar chart: requests rise every day, from 1,200 on Monday to 3,400 on Saturday.">
  </div>
  <figcaption class="cai-figure__caption">Requests per day, last six days.</figcaption>

How it works

The native <figure> and <figcaption>, styled by components/figure.css. No JavaScript.

  • The caption names the figure for assistive technology.
  • A bare <figure> with no class also gets a simple style from the base elements.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: a figure is HTML and CSS.

Keyboard and ARIA

A figure is not interactive and takes no focus.

  • The media needs its own text alternative: alt on an <img>, or role="img" and aria-label on an inline <svg>.
  • A chart also needs its numbers as text: in the alt text, the caption or a table next to it.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use a figure for a chart, an image, a diagram or a code sample that the text refers to and that needs a caption.

When not to use

Do not use a figure for decoration.

  • A decorative image gets alt="" and no figure.
  • For a picture in a box of fixed shape, use the platform image.

Do and don't

Writing the content

The caption says what the figure shows, and for what period or scope.

  • The alt text says what the reader would see: the trend, not "chart".
  • Do not repeat the caption in the alt text.

Contrast and focus

The caption is secondary text on the figure's surface.

Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings, borders and icons.

  • Focus: the figure takes none.
  • Graphics: the colours inside the media are yours: keep the parts of a chart that carry meaning at 3:1 against their background (WCAG 1.4.11). The brand start border is decorative.
