.o-shell, .o-main and .o-container are page-level: this page uses them.

Pick an object

Objects only lay things out: no colour, no type. Spacing comes from the space tokens.

ClassWhat it does
.o-stack
A column, 12 px between items
.o-row
A row that wraps, items centred vertically, 12 px between them
.o-grid
A grid with 16 px gaps; set the columns with your own grid-template-columns, or with one of the modifiers below
.o-grid--auto
As many equal columns as fit, none narrower than --luz-grid-min (16 rem by default): the grid reflows by itself and needs no breakpoint
.o-grid--2, .o-grid--3, .o-grid--4
That many equal columns above 768 px; one column at 768 px and below
.o-grid--split
Two equal columns when each gets --luz-grid-min (16 rem by default), one below: a hero with its image, or two features side by side. It stacks by itself with larger text
.o-media, .o-media__body
A fixed item, such as an avatar, beside content that takes the remaining width
.o-container
The content width (--luz-content-max, 1100 px) and page padding; less padding at 640 px and below
.o-container--inline
The container's width and side gutters only, with no padding above or below: the row of a bar, so its content starts where the page's content starts
.o-page
A page without a sidebar: a column at least as tall as the window, whose <main> grows, so the footer rests on the bottom of a short page. Any number of bands can come before the header
.o-sticky
A bar that stays at the top while the page scrolls, in a window at least 30 rem tall, with the scroll padding that keeps what Tab reaches out from under it
.o-actions
Two or three related buttons or links in a row that wraps, 8 px apart; at 640 px and below, a column of full-width actions whose labels wrap
.o-shell
The app layout: a sidebar column (--luz-sidebar-width) beside the main area, a footer across the bottom; one column at 768 px and below
.o-main
The main area inside .o-shell

The page and its bars

A page without a sidebar is .o-page: any bands first (a notice, the cookie question), then the header, <main> and the footer. The header's row is .o-container.o-container--inline, so the site's name starts where the content starts, and .o-sticky keeps the header at the top.

HTML 
<body>
  <a class="luz-skip-link" href="#main-content">Skip to main content</a>
  <div class="o-page">
    <header class="o-sticky">
      <div class="o-container o-container--inline o-row">…</div>
    </header>
    <main id="main-content" class="o-container o-stack" tabindex="-1">…</main>
    <footer>
      <div class="o-container o-container--inline o-row">…</div>
    </footer>
  </div>
</body>
  • The objects lay out only: the header's surface and divider are the job of your CSS, or of the header of a package for one kind of site.
  • --luz-bar-h is the height of a one-row bar of 44 px controls at the default text size: the control, two 4 px paddings and a 1 px divider. The navbar panel opens at that height when nothing anchors it.
  • With .o-sticky on the page, the scroll padding of <html> is --luz-bar-h or 3.5em, whichever is more, plus half a control: a field reached with Tab, or the target of the skip link, stops below the bar. It is a minimum: a bar that wraps to several rows with enlarged text is taller, and Safari can leave a field partly under it at 300 percent text.
  • Below 30 rem of window height the bar scrolls away with the page, so it never takes most of a short screen.

  • One primary action per group, and a second, outline one if there is another way. In the narrow column the primary comes first, as in the source.
  • A .luz-btn in .o-actions wraps its label in the narrow column, so a long label never runs out of the window.

The grid

LUZ has no 12-column system: a content grid is a handful of equal columns, and .o-grid draws them. Use .o-grid--auto when the items decide how many fit, such as cards; use .o-grid--2 to --4 when the number is part of the design.

HTML 
<!-- as many columns as fit, each at least 16 rem -->
<div class="o-grid o-grid--auto">…cards…</div>

<!-- at least 12 rem: set the property in your own CSS -->
<div class="o-grid o-grid--auto gallery">…</div>
<style>.gallery { --luz-grid-min: 12rem; }</style>

<!-- three columns above 768 px, one below -->
<div class="o-grid o-grid--3">…</div>
  • Items keep their source order: the grid reorders nothing, so reading order and tab order are the page's.
  • Columns are minmax(0, 1fr), so a long word or a wide table inside an item cannot push the grid wider than its container.
  • For a layout that depends on the width of a card and not of the window, put the grid in a container and size the items there; the objects do not read the container.

Lists

When the items are a list (posts, speakers, tags), put .o-grid, .o-stack or .o-row on the <ul> or <ol> itself. The object takes off the markers and the indent, and the items stay a list: a screen reader says how many there are and lets the reader skip past them.

HTML 
<ul class="o-grid o-grid--auto">
  <li class="luz-card">…</li>
  <li class="luz-card">…</li>
</ul>
  • Safari can drop the list role from a list without markers, outside a <nav>. Give each item a heading, as cards have, so the reader can still move through them.
  • No rule of yours is needed. Inside prose, which gives lists their markers back, a list with an object keeps none.
