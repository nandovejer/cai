Known issues

Captions, transcripts and audio description depend on the media you provide. The colours of the captions are a request: the reader's own caption settings win, and a browser may draw them its own way, in its own full-screen player for example. A MIDI file has no player without JavaScript: offer a download link in the fallback text.

How it works

The native media element plays the file; player.js drives the CAI controls, styled by components/player.css.

  • initPlayers() mounts every .cai-player on the page, or inside the element you pass it. cai.js runs it. A player already mounted is left as it is, so running it again is safe.
  • On mount, the script removes the native controls and binds the CAI ones: play and pause, time, seek and volume. They start from the media's own state: a muted video shows as muted, and a length the browser already knows shows at once.
  • Seek and volume are native <input type="range"> sliders. Once the length is known, the seek slider has one step per second, whatever its max in the markup.
  • A full-screen or picture-in-picture button is hidden where the browser cannot do it.
  • Captions and subtitles are the browser's: each <track> is a text track, and the browser draws its cues. The CAI controls only set which track shows, so a choice made in the browser's own menu shows in them too. ::cue gives the cues light text on the media surface. Nothing is stored. A control with no track to show is hidden.
  • mountPlayer(root) and mountMidiPlayer(root) mount one player.

Without JavaScript

Audio and video show the browser's own controls, and the CAI controls stay hidden. The browser's controls offer the captions and subtitles, and a track with default shows from the start. A MIDI player shows its fallback text instead.

Keyboard and ARIA

Tab moves through the controls; each one keeps its native keys.

  • Buttons: Enter or Space. Their names change with the state: "Play" or "Pause", "Mute" or "Unmute". The captions button keeps its name, "Captions", and says whether it is pressed (aria-pressed).
  • Track select: the browser's own keys; each option is in the language of its track.
  • Sliders: arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down. An arrow key moves the seek slider one second, and the volume slider one step (5 % in the example). The seek slider says the time as text.
  • When the player itself has focus, not one of its controls (after a click on the video, for example): Space or K plays and pauses, and M mutes. F goes full screen and C turns captions on or off when the player has those buttons. With Ctrl, Alt or Cmd held, these keys do nothing.
  • The player is a role="group" named by its aria-label.

Design

When to use

Use a player to play a video, a sound or a MIDI file inside the page, when the reader starts it.

When not to use

Do not use a player for media that starts on its own or plays in the background.

  • Never autoplay.
  • For a short animated illustration with no sound, use an image or a figure.

Do and don't

Writing the content

Name each player by what it plays: "Episode 12 player", not "Audio player".

  • Give video captions (<track kind="captions">) and a transcript when it has speech. Captions include the sounds as well as the words; subtitles (kind="subtitles") translate the words.
  • Label each track in its own language: "English", "Español".
  • Describe a silent video in its aria-label or next to it.
  • Say the length and the format next to the player when it is large.
