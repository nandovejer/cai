Known issues

In high contrast the done part and the track are only 1.5:1 apart, under the 3:1 of WCAG 1.4.11 (Non-text contrast) when the bar alone gives the value. Write the figure in words next to the bar, as in the example. Listed in the accessibility statement.

How it works

The native <progress> element, styled by components/progress.css: an 8 px bar, full width, with the brand colour as the fill.

  • The browser exposes the role and the value to assistive technology.
  • In Chromium and WebKit browsers the fill slides to a new value; under reduced motion it jumps.
  • In forced colors the bar keeps a border and draws the fill in the system highlight colour.

Without JavaScript

The bar shows the value in the markup. Moving it needs your script, which sets value.

Keyboard and ARIA

A progress bar is not interactive and takes no focus.

  • Name it with a <label>, or an aria-label when no label is shown.
  • Screen readers do not announce every change. Announce the start and the end in a polite live region (role="status").

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use a progress bar for a task that takes more than a few seconds, such as an upload or a build.

When not to use

Do not use a progress bar for a value that is not a task.

  • For a measure in a known range, such as storage used, use a <meter> (form fields).
  • Do not use it as decoration.

Do and don't

Writing the content

Say what is happening, not just the number: "Uploading report.pdf".

  • Put the figure in the name or next to the bar: "Upload, 25%".
  • When it ends, say so: "Upload complete".

Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings, borders and icons.

  • Focus: the bar takes none.
  • Not colour alone: put the figure in words next to the bar or in its name, so the value never depends on telling the two parts of the bar apart.
