Example

Since March 2016, a group of volunteers has read the tide gauge in the port at six in the morning and six in the evening. Their sheets are the longest record of the sea in the bay that anyone has kept by hand, and the council will publish them as a table of open data.

How the readings were taken

Each reading is the height of the water against the gauge, to the nearest centimetre. The sheet calls it level_cm.

  • Two readings a day, every day of the year.
  • 41 volunteers over ten years.
  • Fewer than one reading in a hundred left out.

We did not set out to measure the climate. We wanted to know when to take the boats out.

Four centimetres in ten years

The average height at high tide was 4 cm higher in 2025 than in 2016, close to what the national gauges record along the coast.

Known issues

A table without a wrapper that is wider than the column pushes the page sideways on a narrow screen. Put a wide table in core's table wrapper, .cai-table-wrap, which scrolls inside itself. Not yet tested with a screen reader.

How it works

CSS only, in components/prose.css. Every selector is inside :where(), so a CAI component inside the text, or a rule of yours, wins.

  • Line length. Each block of the wrapper is at most --cai-prose-measure wide. Figures, tables, code blocks, images, videos and .cai-table-wrap take the whole width of the wrapper, never more.
  • Space between blocks. A block starts --cai-space-md below the one before it. A heading or a rule starts further down (--cai-space-xl), and the block after a heading sits closer (--cai-space-sm). Margins, not a gap: a component inside keeps its own margin, and the two collapse.
  • Lists. Every list gets its indent and markers back, with a class or without one (an editor's ul.wp-block-list), except a list with a layout object (.o-grid, .o-stack) or a CAI component class.
  • Inline code has the look of .cai-code-inline. Long words and addresses break rather than push the page sideways.
  • Classless mode takes the same three steps between blocks, so the two agree; prose is in a later layer, so it wins where they meet. Prose adds the line length and the lists; classless mode adds the look of tables and controls.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: prose is CSS.

Keyboard and ARIA

Prose takes no focus and adds no role. Tab reaches the links and controls inside it, in source order.

  • A long <pre> scrolls sideways inside itself. To scroll it with the keyboard, give it tabindex="0", role="region" and a name, as core's code block does.
  • The headings inside make the outline of the text: start at the level under the page's title, and do not skip one.

Design

When to use

Use prose around running text whose markup you do not write block by block: the body of a post or a page from a CMS editor, Markdown, a help article.

When not to use

Do not put prose around a whole page or an interface: the header, the navigation, forms and cards lay themselves out.

  • For a column of blocks you build yourself, use .o-stack.
  • For one or two lines inside a component, such as a card's text, use the component's own classes.

Do and don't

Writing the content

Write for someone who reads the first words of each line and skips the rest.

  • One idea a paragraph. Put the point first.
  • Headings in order, in sentence case, that say what the section is about.
  • A list for three or more things of the same kind; a sentence for two.
  • Links that say where they go, and figures with a caption that says what they show.
