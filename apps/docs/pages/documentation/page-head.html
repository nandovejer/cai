Variants and options

No modifier. The head is a class on a native <header> with four parts.

ClassEffect
.luz-docs-page-head
On a <header> in the content: one column, with a gap between its parts and room under it. Its h1 is --luz-text-4xl, and --luz-text-2xl at 640px and below
.luz-docs-page-head__eyebrow
On a <p>: the area and the group, in small spaced capitals and the secondary colour. A <code> in it, a package name, keeps its own case. In Arabic the spacing between letters is left out
.luz-docs-page-head__lead
On a <p>: one sentence, larger than the text, in the secondary colour
.luz-docs-page-head__meta
On a <ul>: the chips. Each <li> is a word or two in a rounded outline, at least --luz-control-sm (28px) tall. A link in one is the same height
.luz-docs-page-head__files
On a <p>, instead of the chips, on a page that is not a component: "Files:" and each file in a <code>, one small line in the secondary colour

How it works

CSS from @luz-ds/docs. It sets the rhythm and the sizes; the words are yours.

  • The head is a grid of one column. The gap between the parts is --luz-space-sm and the space under the head is --luz-space-xl.
  • The h1 is a plain element inside the head, with no class: the head styles it by position.
  • The chips are a list of facts a reader asks before reading: which files, which script, which modes, whether it is tested, whether it has known issues. A chip that leads somewhere is a link.
  • A long word in a chip breaks instead of widening the page, so the chips wrap at 320px.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes. The head is text and links, and the package has no script.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • Order: the order of the source: eyebrow, title, lead, then the chips. Tab reaches only the links in the chips.
  • Headings: the h1 is the one h1 of the page. A <header> inside <main> is not a banner landmark.
  • Chips: a list of plain items, announced as a list. A chip with no link is text and takes no focus.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use the head at the top of every page of a documentation site, so a reader finds the title and what the page covers in the same place.

  • One sentence that says what the page lets a reader do.
  • A few chips for the facts that decide whether to read on.

When not to use

Do not use the head for the banner of the site: it holds the page's h1.

  • One head per page. A section of a long page has an h2, not another head.
  • For the header of an article in a blog, use the CMS article header.
  • A landing page opens with the hero.

Do and don't

Writing the content

  • Eyebrow: the area, then the group, with a space, a middle dot and a space: "Guides · Setup". Sentence case; the style makes the capitals.
  • Title: what the page is about, or a task that starts with a verb: "Install the command line tool". No full stop.
  • Lead: one sentence that ends with a full stop, in about twenty words or fewer.
  • Chips: two to five, a word or a few. State a fact ("Version 2"), never a slogan. "No known issues" links to the list.
