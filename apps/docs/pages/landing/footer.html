Variants and options

No modifiers. The footer is a class of the landing on a native <footer>, with a row of core's or a grid of a brand and columns inside.

ClassEffect
.luz-landing-footer
On <footer>: a rule on top, 24 px above its row and 32 px below; its row spreads its children to the two ends, and is a column aligned to the start at 768 px and below
.o-container.o-container--inline.o-row (core)
The row: the width and gutters of the page, so its content starts where the main's does
.luz-landing-footer__brand
The first cell of the .o-container.o-container--inline.o-grid (core), 24 px (--luz-space-lg) apart: a name, a version, one line. From 768 px it is twice a column
.luz-landing-footer__col
A column, on a <nav>: a title over a list of links with no markers, 8 px (--luz-space-xs) apart
.luz-landing-footer__col-title
A column's title: sans, small, semibold, primary text
.o-actions (core)
The links, as an action group

How it works

CSS from @luz-ds/landing on core's objects. No JavaScript, and @luz-ds/site is optional.

  • The landing adds the rule, the room around the row and how the row spreads its children; .o-container--inline lines the row up with the page's content.
  • On an .o-page the main grows, so the footer rests on the bottom of a page shorter than the window.
  • With a brand and columns the cells stack, and from 768 px they sit in a row: the brand takes two shares, each column one.
  • The column at 768 px and below does not wrap, so its children stay one under the other.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: the footer is HTML and CSS. A color mode switcher inside it keeps working as radios.

Keyboard and ARIA

The footer has no interaction of its own; its links keep theirs.

  • A <footer> that is a direct child of <body> is the contentinfo landmark.
  • Name a <nav> of links with aria-label, and keep the links in the same order on every page.
  • Name a column's <nav> by its title with aria-labelledby, and make the title a heading (<h2>).

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use the footer at the end of every page of a landing site, for who made it, its licence and a few secondary links.

When not to use

Do not put the main navigation, or anything needed to finish a task, in the footer.

  • Main navigation goes in the header.

Do and don't

Writing the content

Keep it short: who made it, the licence, and two to four links.

  • Each link says where it goes: "Accessibility statement", not "Legal".
  • The brand is the name, a version if people ask which one, and one line on what it is. A column title is one word: "Product", "Company", "Legal".
