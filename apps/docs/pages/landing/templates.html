Each block is complete markup for the <main> of a page: start from the landing page shell, then add the blocks one after another, in the order a visitor reads them. Change the words and the links; keep one <h1>, in the hero, and an <h2> for each block.

They use the classes of core and landing, and site's elements where a search or a language switch goes. Where LUZ has nothing for a part, the last section says so.

Landing page shell

The page of a product or an event: the skip link, the sticky header, the <main> that holds the blocks below, and the footer.

  • .o-page makes the body a column at least as tall as the window, so the footer rests on the bottom of a short page. .luz-landing-glow adds the soft tint in the corner where reading starts; leave it out for a flat page.
  • .luz-landing-main on the <main> spaces the blocks; the hero brings its own room above.
HTML 
<body class="o-page luz-landing-glow">
  <a class="luz-skip-link" href="#main-content">Skip to content</a>

  <header class="luz-landing-header o-sticky">
    <div class="o-container o-container--inline o-row">
      <button class="luz-nav-toggle" type="button" popovertarget="site-nav" aria-label="Menu">…</button>
      <a class="luz-landing-header__brand" href="/">Acme Summit</a>
      <nav class="luz-navbar" id="site-nav" aria-label="Main" popover>
        <ul class="luz-navbar__list">
          <li><a class="luz-navbar__link" href="/" aria-current="page">Summit</a></li>

        </ul>
      </nav>

Hero with an image

The first block of an event page: what it is, when and where, the main action and a picture. It is the hero with its image.

The text comes first in the source, so it comes first for a screen reader and when the columns stack.

  • The hero gives two equal columns when each gets 24rem (the hero is 51rem wide or more: from about 900 px of window on a full-width page) and one column below. The picture is core's image frame: it keeps its ratio and crops the picture to it.
  • Give the image its real width and height and an alt that says what it shows, or alt="" if it only decorates. Do not set loading="lazy" on it: it is the first thing on the page.
  • One primary button. The second action is an outline button.

Programme

A schedule is a table: the reader compares times and rooms. Below 480 px of its own width each row becomes a card of label and value pairs, so it needs no sideways scrolling on a phone.

  • One table for each day, with the day in the <caption> and in the wrapper's aria-label. For several days, repeat the table under an <h3> for each day.
  • data-label on every <td> is the name shown before the value in the stacked layout. See Table.
HTML 
<section class="o-stack luz-landing-section" id="programme" aria-labelledby="programme-title">
  <h2 id="programme-title">Programme</h2>
  <div class="luz-table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="Programme, Wednesday 12 May" tabindex="0">
    <table class="luz-table">
      <caption>Wednesday 12 May</caption>
      <thead>
        <tr><th scope="col">Time</th><th scope="col">Session</th><th scope="col">Room</th></tr>

Speakers

A list of people: a photo, the name and what they do. Each speaker is a card with an image frame of ratio 1:1, in a list that fits as many columns as there is room for.

  • The photo has alt="": the name is in the heading beside it and says the same. If a photo shows something the name does not carry, give it an alt that says that, never the name again.
  • The name is an <h3>, one level under the section's <h2>, so people can move through the speakers by headings. The role is the card's body: "Role, Organisation", one line.
  • The <ul> tells a screen reader how many people there are. Core's .o-grid on the list takes off the bullets and the indent. Safari can drop the list role from a list without markers; the headings still lead through the speakers.
  • .o-grid--auto puts as many columns as fit, none narrower than 16rem. Set --luz-grid-min on the list for narrower cards: 12rem gives five across a 1100 px container, and one column below 336 px stays one column.
  • No photo yet: leave the frame empty (<div class="luz-image luz-image--1x1 u-mb-sm"></div>) and it keeps the square as a flat surface. To link a speaker to a page of their own, put a link in the heading, <h3 class="luz-card__title"><a href="/speakers/luis-ortega/">Luis Ortega</a></h3>: the whole card becomes the link. Link all the speakers or none, and do not add a second link or a button.

Ticket plans

Three plans side by side above 768 px, one under another below it. Each plan is a card with its name, its price, what is included and one action.

  • .o-grid--3 gives three equal columns above 768 px. For two or four plans use .o-grid--2 or .o-grid--4, or .o-grid--auto to let the width decide.
  • .luz-card__price sets the price in semibold figures of equal width; a previous price is an <s> with a .u-sr-only "Was" before it, so the line through it is not the only cue.
  • The button says which plan it chooses ("Choose Two days"), so a list of buttons makes sense out of context. The recommended plan is a featured card with the primary button and a tag that says why, in its text; the others are outline buttons.

Call to action

A reason to act now and one action, repeated near the end of a long page. It is a section on core's band: the tint runs from edge to edge of the page, and the heading, one sentence and the action group keep the page's column.

  • One primary button, and it points at the form or the page where the visitor acts (#register). The heading says why now, not "Register".
  • Use a heading level that follows the page: <h2> here, because it sits beside the other sections.
  • One band for each screen height at most, and not inside a card. In forced colors the tint is removed and two thin rules mark the band.
HTML 
<section class="o-stack luz-landing-section luz-band" aria-labelledby="last-seats-title">
  <h2 id="last-seats-title">Registration closes on 30 April</h2>
  <p>The hall holds 400 people, and the Two days ticket sold out last year.</p>
  <div class="o-actions">
    <a class="luz-btn luz-btn--primary" href="#register">Register now</a>
  </div>
</section>

Registration form

The words on the left, the form on the right above 768 px; one column below. It is a native <form> that works without JavaScript, with the constraint validation of the browser.

  • Every field has a visible label. Say once, above the form, what "*" means. The hint is linked with aria-describedby.
  • An error says what went wrong and how to fix it; the markup is on Form fields.
  • The consent is a checkbox with a label and a link to the notice, not a sentence under the button. The button says what happens: "Complete registration", not "Submit".
  • action and the field names are yours: the form is sent to your server.
HTML

Product page with a centred hero

The page of a product rather than an event: a centred hero with a picture to the edges of the window, a section with a head and three stats, and a footer with a brand and columns. It goes inside the shell, in place of its <main> and footer.

  • The hero is one column at every width. Its picture has the class __media--bleed, so it runs past the gutters of the page; keep .o-container on the <main>, as the bleed needs a column centred in the window.
  • The head is an eyebrow, a title and a lead, and the stats are a list on core's grid. Set --luz-landing-title-accent on the <main> to colour the <em> of both titles.
  • The footer's columns are navigation: each is a <nav> named by its title.

What is not here

An event page often asks for things LUZ does not have. They are not faked in the templates above.

  • A countdown or a map. LUZ draws neither. For icons, LUZ has its own set to copy.
