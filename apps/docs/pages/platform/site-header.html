At 768 px of window width and below the links collapse into a menu. Its panel opens under the top of the window, where a header sits, not under this example.

Known issues

If you keep pressing Tab after the last link of the open menu, focus moves on into the page and the menu stays open over it, hiding what is behind. Escape closes it. This meets WCAG 2.4.11 (AA) and does not meet 2.4.12 (AAA). The panel opens under the header's height at the default text size: with enlarged text and a header that wraps to a second row, it can cover that row.

How it works

CSS from @cai-ds/platform and a native popover. No JavaScript.

  • The <nav> is a popover and the menu button its popovertarget. At 769 px and above CSS shows it as a row, and the button is hidden; at 768 px and below it is a panel as wide as the window, under the header. The browser gives Escape, light dismiss (a click outside), focus return to the button and the expanded state.
  • The icon in the button is an inline SVG painted with currentcolor, so it stays visible in forced-colors mode. Core's .cai-nav-toggle__bar spans work too, since core paints them in ButtonText there.
  • The panel and the language list open under --cai-platform-site-header-h: a one-row header of 44 px controls. It is exact at the default text size. If your header is taller (enlarged text wraps the row, or you add a second row), set the variable on the header yourself.
  • --sticky applies only to windows at least 30rem tall, and sets scroll-padding-block-start on the page to at least 3.5rem or the header's height, so a field reached with Tab is never left under it. On a short window, such as a phone turned sideways, a sticky header would take too much of the screen.
  • The scrim of the open panel starts under the header, so the header's controls keep their contrast.
  • Without the popover API there is no panel: the links stay in the row and wrap.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: the popover is opened by the button's popovertarget, with no script, and the links are plain links.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • Order: the menu button, the brand, the links, then the controls. With the menu open, the next Tab after the button goes into the panel's first link, then the others, then the brand and the controls: the browser places an open popover right after its button.
  • Keys: Enter or Space on the button opens and closes the panel. Escape closes it from anywhere and, from a link in it, returns focus to the button. Tab and Shift+Tab move through the links as usual.
  • Names: the <nav> has an aria-label ("Main") so it is told apart from a footer navigation. The button's name is "Menu" (its icon is decorative); the browser exposes its expanded state, so write no aria-expanded. The current link has aria-current="page" and an underline.
  • Landmarks: a <header> that is a direct child of <body> is the banner. Put the skip link before it.
  • Open panel and focus: pressing Tab past the last link of the open panel moves focus on, and the panel stays open. See Known issues.

Design

When to use

Use a site header at the top of every page of a site with more than one section: the visitor sees which site this is and reaches its main sections from any page.

When not to use

Do not use it on a single page that fits one screen: a heading and a skip link are enough.

  • For an app whose main navigation is a column of sections, use the sidebar and keep the header to the name and the account.
  • Do not put two headers on a page, and do not put it inside a component: it is the page's banner.
  • For the title of the page, use the page header under it.

Do and don't

Writing the content

The brand is the site's name; the links are the names of its main sections.

  • Brand: the name as people say it, one to three words. The logo is decorative: the name stays as text in the link.
  • Links: three to six, one or two words each, the same words as the titles of the pages they go to. No "Home" beside a brand that already goes home.
  • Menu button: "Menu". Translate it like the rest of the page.
