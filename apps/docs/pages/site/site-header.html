At 768 px of window width and below the links collapse into a menu. Its panel opens under this example where the browser supports CSS anchor positioning, and under the top of the window, where a header sits, elsewhere.

Known issues

If you keep pressing Tab after the last link of the open menu, focus moves on into the page and the menu stays open over it, hiding what is behind. Escape closes it. This meets WCAG 2.4.11 (AA) and does not meet 2.4.12 (AAA). Where CSS anchor positioning is supported (Chrome and Edge 125, Safari 26, Firefox 147) the panel opens under the header wherever it ends, even with a notice or the cookie banner above it. Elsewhere it opens at the header's height at the default text size, from the top of the window: with enlarged text and a header that wraps to a second row, or with a band above the header before the page scrolls, it can cover part of the header. Escape or a click outside closes it.

  • Sticky header and enlarged text. The scroll padding is a minimum: at least 3.5em plus half a control. Measured in WebKit on Linux, a field reached with Tab ends 14 px below the header at 100% and 200% text. At 300% text, with the header wrapped to several rows (252 to 353 px tall on the test page), Safari still leaves the field under the header at window widths of 320, 390, 1000, 1024 and 1280 px; Chrome and Edge centre the field and keep it clear. To fix it in your site, set scroll-padding-block-start on html, in em so it grows with the text, to your header's real height when it wraps plus half of --luz-control-lg; a rule outside the luz layers wins. Or drop --sticky if your header wraps at large text.
  • Forced colors in Firefox. The dim layer behind the open menu is not drawn, so the page is not dimmed. The panel keeps its border, so it is still set apart.
  • Breakpoints in Safari on a Mac. With always-visible scrollbars, Safari does not count the scrollbar in width media queries (WebKit bug 52653), so the menu collapses, or the links return to the row, when the window is up to about 15 px wider than 768 px.

How it works

CSS from @luz-ds/site and a native popover. No JavaScript.

  • The <nav> is a popover and the menu button its popovertarget. At 769 px and above CSS shows it as a row, and the button is hidden; at 768 px and below it is a panel as wide as the window, under the header. The browser gives Escape, light dismiss (a click outside), focus return to the button and the expanded state.
  • The icon in the button is an inline SVG painted with currentcolor, so it stays visible in forced-colors mode. Core's .luz-nav-toggle__bar spans work too, since core paints them in ButtonText there.
  • The panel and the language list open under the header: placed against it with CSS anchor positioning where the browser supports it, so a notice or the cookie banner above the header and a row wrapped by enlarged text are taken into account. Elsewhere they open at --luz-site-header-h from the top of the window: a one-row header of 44 px controls, exact at the default text size. If your header is taller, set the variable on the header yourself.
  • --sticky applies only to windows at least 30rem tall, and sets scroll-padding-block-start on the page to at least 3.5rem or the header's height, plus half a control (--luz-control-lg), so a field reached with Tab is not left under it. See Known issues for enlarged text. On a short window, such as a phone turned sideways, a sticky header would take too much of the screen.
  • The scrim of the open panel starts under the header, so the header's controls keep their contrast.
  • A link with more links under it is a submenu: a native <details> in the list, under its button in the panel and a box under it in the row.
  • Without the popover API there is no panel: the links stay in the row and wrap.

Without JavaScript

Nothing changes: the popover is opened by the button's popovertarget, with no script, and the links are plain links.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • Order: the menu button, the brand, the links, then the controls. With the menu open, the next Tab after the button goes into the panel's first link, then the others, then the brand and the controls: the browser places an open popover right after its button.
  • Keys: Enter or Space on the button opens and closes the panel. Escape closes it from anywhere and, from a link in it, returns focus to the button. Tab and Shift+Tab move through the links as usual.
  • Names: the <nav> has an aria-label ("Main") so it is told apart from a footer navigation. The button's name is "Menu" (its icon is decorative); the browser exposes its expanded state, so write no aria-expanded. The current link has aria-current="page" and an underline.
  • Landmarks: a <header> that is a direct child of <body> is the banner. Put the skip link before it.
  • Open panel and focus: pressing Tab past the last link of the open panel moves focus on, and the panel stays open. See Known issues.

Design

When to use

Use a site header at the top of every page of a site with more than one section: the visitor sees which site this is and reaches its main sections from any page.

When not to use

Do not use it on a single page that fits one screen: a heading and a skip link are enough.

  • For an app whose main navigation is a column of sections, use the sidebar and keep the header to the name and the account.
  • Do not put two headers on a page, and do not put it inside a component: it is the page's banner.
  • For the title of the page, use the page header under it.

Do and don't

Writing the content

The brand is the site's name; the links are the names of its main sections.

  • Brand: the name as people say it, one to three words. The logo is decorative: the name stays as text in the link.
  • Links: three to six, one or two words each, the same words as the titles of the pages they go to. No "Home" beside a brand that already goes home.
  • Menu button: "Menu". Translate it like the rest of the page.
