        For one kind of site · @cai-ds/cms

        What a blog, a newspaper or any site of articles needs on top of site, from the article header to the post listing, and how to tell whether you need it.

        A site of articles publishes many pages of the same shape: a story with its header, its text and its author, and lists that lead to them. This package holds the patterns only that kind of site uses. Everything every site needs, from the page shell to the site header, stays in Site.

        • It works with any CMS and any stack. The classes go on any element, so a template of WordPress, Drupal, Ghost, Hugo or your own code can write them.
        • Every class starts with .cai-cms-, uses semantic tokens only and sits in the cascade layer cai.cms, after site's.
        • It has no script. Load cms.css after the CSS of tokens, core and site: Install CAI has the command and the CDN tags. With core's script, the table of contents marks the section in view.
        • It has a version of its own, 0.x while it grows, and needs version 3 of tokens, core and site, declared as peer dependencies.
        • The post card, the post listing, related posts and the author box need no CSS of their own: they are core's card, avatar and pagination, put together on CMS templates.

        Use cms when

        • You publish articles, posts or stories, each with a title, an author and a date.
        • Your articles are long enough to need a table of contents beside the text.

        Use site alone when

        • Your pages are documentation, a landing page or an app.
        • You have a few pages with no author or date: the page header is enough.

        Patterns

        • Article header

          Opens a story with its section, its title, a standfirst, who wrote it and when, and an optional lead picture.

        • Table of contents

          Lists the sections of a long article, beside the text when there is room and above it when there is not.

        Reference

        • CMS templates The pieces of a blog or a newspaper that need no CSS of their own, to paste in and adapt: the article page, a post card, post listings, related posts and the author box.
