        Table of contents

        .cai-cms-article __layout .cai-cms-toc __title __list

        Lists the sections of a long article, beside the text when there is room and above it when there is not.

        Example

        Contents

        1. How the readings were taken
        2. Four centimetres in ten years
        3. Storms that reach the walls
        4. What the council plans next

        Since March 2016, a group of volunteers has read the tide gauge in the port at six in the morning and six in the evening, every day of the year.

        How the readings were taken

        Each reading is the height of the water against the gauge, to the nearest centimetre, with the time, the wind and the state of the sea. Two people read it, and a third checks the sheet the same week.

        Four centimetres in ten years

        The average height at high tide was 4 cm higher in 2025 than in 2016. That is close to the rise that the national tide gauges record along the whole coast.

        Storms that reach the walls

        In the first five winters, waves reached the sea wall of the old town twice. In the last five, they reached it eleven times.

        What the council plans next

        The council will publish the full records as open data in January and has asked the volunteers to add a third daily reading.

        Beside the text, at the end, when the article is 46rem wide or more; above it below that. With core's script, the section in view is marked as you scroll.

        Known issues

        The mark on the section in view needs core's script; without it, no link is marked. Beside the text, the contents sit at the inline end but come first in the source, so Tab reaches them before the text. Not yet tested with a screen reader.

        Variants and options

        There are no modifiers. The contents are optional: without them, the text takes the first column alone.

        Class or propertyWhat it does
        .cai-cms-article
        The story: its width decides the layout, not the window's (a size container)
        .cai-cms-article__layout
        The contents, then the text. When the article is 46rem wide or more: the text in the first column, the contents in a 13rem column at the inline end, 48 px apart; one column below that
        .cai-cms-toc
        The <nav>. Beside the text it stays in view while the text scrolls, and a list taller than the window scrolls inside it
        data-cai-spy on .cai-cms-toc
        Core's scroll spy marks the link of the section in view. Optional
        .cai-cms-toc__title
        The heading of the contents, in the sans, semibold
        .cai-cms-toc__list
        The list, with no markers and a rule along its inline start; one link a line
        --cai-cms-toc-top
        The space above the contents while they stay in view: 24 px, or 24 px under site's sticky header (.cai-site-header--sticky). Set it on .cai-cms-toc for a header of your own

        How it works

        A CSS grid inside a size container, from @cai-ds/cms. No JavaScript of its own.

        • The layout follows the room the article has: in a page with a sidebar, or with larger text, the contents go above the text sooner. Below 46rem they are a plain list above the text, and do not stay in view: they would cover it.
        • The contents come first in the source, so they are read first and Tab reaches them first, in both layouts. The text is one element, the second child of __layout; in a CMS, the element that holds the post's content.
        • Each link points at the id of a section heading. Most CMSs can write those ids: in WordPress, the HTML anchor of each heading block.
        • With core's script (cai.js, or initSidebar() from @cai-ds/core/sidebar), the link of the section in view gets aria-current="true", which draws a bar and makes it semibold. The script finds the links by the data-cai-spy attribute on the <nav>: every link to a section of the page inside it.

        Without JavaScript

        The contents are a list of links to the sections, which the browser scrolls to. Only the mark on the section in view needs the script; without it, no link is marked.

        Keyboard and ARIA

        Tab moves through the links in order; Enter follows one. The browser moves focus and scrolls to the section.

        • A <nav> named by its heading: screen reader users find it in the list of landmarks as "Contents". Use an <ol>: the sections have an order.
        • aria-current="true" names the section in view; the script moves it. Without the script, nothing claims to be current.
        • Under a sticky header, site's scroll padding keeps the section's heading clear of the header when a link is followed.

        Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

        Design

        When to use

        Use a table of contents in an article of four or more sections, which people read in parts or come back to: a guide, a report, a long interview.

        When not to use

        Do not use it in a short post: three paragraphs need no map.

        • Not for links to other pages: that is navigation, in the site header or a sidebar.
        • Not for the steps of a task done in order: number the steps in the text.

        Do and don't

        Writing the content

        Each link is the text of its section's heading, word for word, so the reader recognises where it leads.

        • List the <h2> of the article only. A list of more than about eight is a sign the article could be two.
        • Call it "Contents", in the reader's language ("Contenido" in Spanish).
