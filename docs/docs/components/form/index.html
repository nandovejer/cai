        Variants and options

        Every control is a native element with a LUZ class. The state comes from native attributes.

        Class or attributeEffect
        .luz-field
        Wraps one label, its control, its hint and its error
        .luz-field__label
        The visible label
        .luz-field__label--required
        Adds a red " *" after the label
        .luz-field__helper
        A hint under the control
        .luz-field__error
        An error under the control, in the danger colour
        .luz-input
        Text, email, number, date, time, colour, file and range inputs and <textarea>; 44 px high
        .luz-input--error
        A 2 px danger underline; pair it with aria-invalid="true"
        .luz-input--textarea
        Auto height, at least 80 px, resizes vertically
        .luz-select
        A <select>, on its own page: Select
        .luz-check-group, .luz-check-item
        A list of checkboxes or radios, each with its label
        .luz-fieldset
        A bordered group with an upper-case <legend>
        .luz-toggle
        A switch: a checkbox with role="switch" drawn as a track and a thumb
        .luz-meter
        A <meter> bar, in the brand colour in every state: see Meter
        .luz-output
        A computed value
        disabled, readonly
        Faded; read-only also gets a dashed underline

        How it works

        Native form controls, styled by components/form.css. No JavaScript: the browser owns the state, the keyboard, validation and submission.

        • Each control gets its name from its <label>; the hint and the error are tied to it with aria-describedby.
        • LUZ does not mark a field as invalid for you: add .luz-input--error, aria-invalid="true" and the error text when your validation fails.
        • Field text is 16 px at 768 px and below and on touch screens, so iOS Safari does not zoom in.

        Without JavaScript

        Every control works and submits with its form, including the switch. Only what your own script adds is missing, such as live validation or a value echoed in an <output>.

        Keyboard and ARIA

        The native keys apply. Tab moves between controls.

        • Space checks a checkbox or a switch.
        • Arrow keys choose within a radio group, and move a range slider.
        • Group related controls in a <fieldset> with a <legend>, or a role="group" named with aria-labelledby.
        • Write "Error: " before the error text, hidden visually with .u-sr-only if you like.

        Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

        Design

        When to use

        Use these fields to collect input, with the native control that fits the data.

        • Text, email, number, date, time: an input of that type.
        • One choice from a few (six or fewer): radios. From many: a select.
        • Several choices: checkboxes. A setting that applies at once: a switch.

        When not to use

        Do not rebuild a native control from <div> elements.

        • Do not use a placeholder as the label: it disappears when the reader types.
        • Do not use a switch inside a form that is sent with a button: use a checkbox.
        • To show how far a task has got, use progress, not a meter.

        Do and don't

        Writing the content

        Every control has a visible label that names what to enter.

        • Labels are short nouns, in sentence case: "Project name".
        • A hint, under the control, says the format or why you ask.
        • An error says what is wrong and how to fix it: "Use letters, numbers and dashes only."
        • If you mark required fields with "*", say once, above the form, what it means.
