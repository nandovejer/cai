        Hero

        .luz-landing-hero --centered __copy __eyebrow __title __lead __media __media--bleed

        Opens a home or landing page with its title, one sentence, the main actions and an optional picture.

        Copy the markup

        HTML 
        <section class="luz-landing-hero o-grid o-grid--split" aria-labelledby="hero-title">
  <div class="luz-landing-hero__copy">
    <p class="luz-landing-hero__eyebrow">12 and 13 May 2027 · Seville</p>
    <h1 class="luz-landing-hero__title" id="hero-title">Acme Summit 2027</h1>
    <p class="luz-landing-hero__lead">Two days of talks and workshops for retail teams.</p>
    <div class="o-actions">
      <a class="luz-btn luz-btn--primary" href="#register">Register</a>
      <a class="luz-btn luz-btn--outline" href="#programme">See the programme</a>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="luz-landing-hero__media">
    <div class="luz-image luz-image--4x3">
      <img src="/img/venue-1280.jpg" width="1280" height="960" alt="The main hall, full, before the opening talk" fetchpriority="high" decoding="async">
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

        Variants and options

        Two modifiers, --centered and __media--bleed, as in the product page template. The eyebrow, the lead and the picture are optional; with no picture the copy takes the whole width. The block is a class of the landing on core's split grid.

        ClassWhat it does
        .luz-landing-hero
        On a <section> with core's .o-grid.o-grid--split: space above it, the gaps, and columns of at least 24rem (--luz-grid-min), so two equal columns when each gets 24rem, one below
        .luz-landing-hero--centered
        One column at every width: the copy at most 48rem wide, centred, with its text and actions centred, and the picture under it
        .luz-landing-hero__copy
        The text and the actions, first in the source
        .luz-landing-hero__eyebrow
        A short line above the title, in small capitals of the mono face; the sans in Arabic
        .luz-landing-hero__title
        The title: heading font, 4xl size, balanced lines; 3xl below 1024 px and 2xl at 640 px and below
        .luz-landing-hero__lead
        One or two sentences in the secondary text colour, 70 characters wide at most; xl, lg below 1024 px and base at 640 px and below
        .luz-landing-hero__media
        The second column, holding core's image frame
        .luz-landing-hero__media--bleed
        On the picture of a centred hero: it runs from edge to edge of the window, past the page's gutters, with no sideways scroll. The frame is 16:9 unless you give it a ratio

        How it works

        A <section> named by its title, laid out by core's split grid and styled by @luz-ds/landing. No JavaScript, and @luz-ds/site is optional.

        • The columns are core's split grid, intrinsic: they follow the room the hero has, not the window. A hero as wide as the page splits from about 900 px of window; in a narrow column, or with larger text, it stays one column.
        • The order is the source order: the copy comes first, on the left in a left-to-right page and on the right in a right-to-left one.
        • The actions are core's action group, .o-actions: at 640 px and below its buttons stack and take the whole width.
        • The bleed measures the window without a viewport unit, which counts a scrollbar and scrolls the page sideways: the page's root becomes a size container (container-type: inline-size). The column must be centred in the window, as core's .o-container is.
        • The em in a title takes the colour of --luz-landing-title-accent, the title's own when it is unset.
        • The classes change the look only. The title must be the page's <h1>.

        Without JavaScript

        Nothing changes: the hero is HTML and CSS.

        Keyboard and ARIA

        The hero has no interaction of its own: Tab reaches its links in the order of the source.

        • aria-labelledby on the <section> points at the title, so the hero is a region with the page's name.
        • Keep it first inside <main>, so the skip link lands right before it.

        Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

        Design

        When to use

        Use a hero at the top of a home page, a landing page or a front page, to say in a few words what the site or the event is and what to do first.

        When not to use

        Do not use it on a page whose reader came to read or to do a task: use a plain <h1> and a lead paragraph, which are smaller.

        • One hero per page, and only at the top.
        • Not for a message in the middle of a page: use a section, with core's band if it must stand out.

        Do and don't

        Writing the content

        The title says what the site or the event is, in sentence case and in fewer than ten words.

        • The eyebrow is a fact the reader needs at once (a date and a place, a product name), not a slogan. Leave it out if there is none.
        • The lead is one or two short sentences: what the reader gets.
        • Buttons start with a verb and say where they go: "Register", "See the programme".
        • The picture's alt says what it shows, or is empty if it only decorates. It is the first thing on the page: no loading="lazy".

        Contrast and focus

        The title is primary text, and the eyebrow and the lead secondary text, on the page. The buttons are core's.

        Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings, borders and icons.

        • Focus: the hero takes none; its buttons keep the focus ring of the button.
        • Accent: the em of a title has the title's colour unless you set --luz-landing-title-accent. The colour is yours: check it against the page surface in every mode you ship, as text needs 4.5:1.
        • Do not put the text over the picture: its contrast would depend on the picture, which no token controls.
