        Main and sections

        .luz-landing-main .luz-landing-section .luz-landing-glow

        Gives a landing page its rhythm: wide room between the blocks of the main, a closer one inside each section, and an optional glow behind the page.

        Variants and options

        Three classes of the landing, each on top of a core object. A section stands out on core's band.

        ClassEffect
        .luz-landing-main
        On main.o-container.o-stack: 64 px (--luz-space-3xl) between the blocks and under the last one; none above the first, as the hero brings its own
        .luz-landing-section
        On section.o-stack: 16 px (--luz-space-md) between its children
        .luz-landing-glow
        On the .o-page element: the brand's subtle tint in the corner where reading starts, over a fade from the page surface to the muted one; none in print

        How it works

        CSS from @luz-ds/landing on core's objects. No JavaScript, and @luz-ds/site is optional.

        • Core's .o-container gives the main its width and gutters, and .o-stack the column; the landing's layer comes after core's objects, so its gaps win over the stack's.
        • The glow's tint sits top left in a left-to-right page and top right in a right-to-left one: a gradient's position is physical, so the class flips it with :dir(rtl).
        • Use a section on a <section> when the group has its own heading, or on a <div> when it does not.

        Without JavaScript

        Nothing changes: the main, the sections and the glow are CSS.

        Keyboard and ARIA

        None of the three has an interaction or a role of its own.

        • The <main> is the main landmark, and the target of the skip link: give it id and tabindex="-1".
        • A <section> named with aria-labelledby becomes a region landmark; name only the sections people would jump to.
        • The glow carries no meaning: without it (forced colors, print) nothing is lost.

        Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

        Design

        When to use

        Use the main and its sections for every page of a landing site, so the space between blocks is the same everywhere. Use the glow on a short page, such as a home page or a sign-up page, to give it some warmth.

        When not to use

        Do not use a section to space the paragraphs of running text: the text styles already do.

        • For blocks side by side, use core's grid objects, such as ul.o-grid.o-grid--split for a few features of equal weight.
        • Do not use the glow on a long page or behind a band: the tint competes with the band's.

        Do and don't

        Writing the content

        Start each section with a heading that says what the section is for.

        • Use the next heading level after the page title: <h2> under the hero's <h1>.
        • Keep one idea per section; a second idea is a second section.

        Contrast and focus

        The main and the sections are spacing only: they add no colour. The glow puts the page's text on the brand's subtle tint in one corner, and on the muted surface at the bottom.

        Tokens it uses

        The 5 tokens that the .luz-landing-main, .luz-landing-section and .luz-landing-glow rules of landing's components/main.css, section.css and glow.css read, by family. Each family links to its page, with the values in every mode. In Figma they are variables with the same names: see Tokens in Figma.
