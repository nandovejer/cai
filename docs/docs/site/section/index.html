        Section

        .luz-site-section --band

        Groups the blocks of one part of a page, with the same space between them everywhere, and sets one of them on a tinted band across the page.

        Example

        Heading
        Lead
        Content

        Children are spaced by --luz-space-md.

        Registration closes on 30 April

        The hall holds 400 people, and the Two days ticket sold out last year.

        Register now

        With .luz-site-section--band the tint runs to the edges of this frame; in a page, to the edges of the window. The content keeps the column.

        Known issues

        The section has none. The band, its one modifier, has three.

        • Band: a tooltip at its top edge is clipped. The band cuts off whatever sticks out above or below it. A CSS tooltip on a control that is the first thing in a band is cut at 200 percent text and larger. Start the band with its heading, and keep tooltips off the first control. Dropdown menus and other popovers are not clipped.
        • Band: outline and ghost buttons. Their hover colour is the band's own tint, so the hover shows no change. The label and the outline still identify them. Use one primary button, and an outline button for a second way.
        • Band: forced colors. The tint is removed, so the section is its heading, text and buttons between two thin rules in the system text colour.

    <h2 id="last-seats-title">Registration closes on 30 April</h2>
    <p>The hall holds 400 people, and the Two days ticket sold out last year.</p>

    </div>
  </section>
</main>

        Variants and options

        One modifier, for a band of tint across the page.

        ClassEffect
        .luz-site-section
        A one-column grid with 16 px (--luz-space-md) between its children
        .luz-site-section--band
        The section on a --luz-brand-subtle band that runs from edge to edge of the page, with 48 px (--luz-space-2xl) above and below its content. The content keeps the page's column

        How it works

        CSS grid from @luz-ds/site. No JavaScript.

        • Inside .luz-site-main, sections are 64 px apart; inside a section, blocks are 16 px apart.
        • Use it on a <section> when the group has its own heading, or on a <div> when it does not.
        • The band stays inside the page's column, so its text keeps the column's left edge. The tint is a box-shadow that spreads past the section, cut off above and below by clip-path. A shadow adds no scrollable width; 100vw would, whenever the page has a scrollbar. In an .o-shell the tint stops at .o-main.
        • Put it inside .luz-site-main, on the page itself. Inside a card or an element with its own background the tint is drawn over that surface, and it runs out of the element when that element is wider than the window.

        Without JavaScript

        Nothing changes: the section is CSS.

        Keyboard and ARIA

        The section has no interaction, and the band adds none.

        • A <section> named with aria-labelledby becomes a region landmark; one without a name does not.
        • Name only the sections people would jump to, or the landmark list gets long.
        • The tint is not what groups the band: the section and its heading are. Without the tint (forced colors, print) the grouping stays.
        • Controls inside the band keep their own focus ring. The 48 px above and below leaves room for it at 200 percent text and at 320 px wide.

        Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

        Design

        When to use

        Use a section to group related blocks in the main area, with the same space between them on every page.

        • Use the band for a call to act near the end of a long page, or for one pause in the middle of it.

        When not to use

        Do not use it to space the paragraphs of running text: the text styles already do.

        • For blocks side by side, use the grid object or the feature grid.
        • Do not band every section: when everything stands out, nothing does. One band for each screen height at most, and two in a row read as a new background, not as a call to act.
        • Do not put a band inside a card or on a --luz-surface area, or around the whole page. In dark mode the primary button falls to 2.7:1 against a tint laid over --luz-surface, under the 3:1 a control's edge needs.
        • Do not use it on a page whose background is already the brand tint: .luz-site-page--gradient starts with it in the top corner.
        • Do not put a .luz-btn--secondary on the band: its fill is the tint's own colour, and it reads as plain text.

        Do and don't

        Writing the content

        Start each section with a heading that says what the section is for.

        • Use the next heading level after the page title: <h2> under the <h1>.
        • On a band, the heading says why now, not the action: "Registration closes on 30 April", not "Register". Add one sentence of support, one primary button and, if you need a second way, one outline button.

        Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings and the edges of controls.

        • Focus: the section takes none; what is inside keeps its own ring, --luz-focus-ring, drawn 4 px out. The band's 48 px of padding keeps it inside the tint.

        • Forced colors: the tint is removed and two 1 px CanvasText rules mark the band's top and bottom.
